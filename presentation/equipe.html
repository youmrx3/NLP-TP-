0 · IntroductionWhat is artificial intelligence, really?

Classic program

A human writes all the rules: "if the message contains 'nul', then it is negative". It works for simple cases, but there are a thousand ways to say "bad".

Machine learning

We do not give the rules: we give examples with the right answer, and the program finds the rules by itself.

The image: teaching a child what a cat is. You do not recite a definition: you show 50 photos saying "cat" or "not a cat". Then you show a new photo and the child guesses. Our project does the same thing with sentences.

0 · IntroductionThe project told as a story

  1. The problem. We want a program that understands the sentiment of Darija messages, but we only have very few reliable examples (36 to learn from).
  2. The idea. An LLM writes 90 extra training sentences, each with its label.
  3. The learning. A small model learns from these sentences.
  4. The exam. We test it on reference sentences (the gold) that it has never seen, so that it cannot cheat.
  5. The verdict. Without the corpus: 35% correct answers (almost chance). With the LLM corpus: 85%. So yes, it helps, with reservations (see the results slides).

2 · The basicsWhy Darija is difficult

① Little data

A low-resource language: very few labelled texts. English has millions of examples, Darija almost none.

② No spelling standard

"mli7a", "mliha", "مليحة" = the same word. Same for "bzaf" / "bezzaf" / "بزاف". A computer thinks they are 3 different words.

③ Code-switching

Arabic and French are mixed:
service top ماشاء الله
Single-language models get lost.

Arabizi digit37952
Arabic letterعحقخء
Example3ayyitmli7a9a3da5edma2alf

2 · The basicsLLM, prompt, few-shot, temperature

LLM

Large Language Model: a very big model trained to predict the continuation of a text (ChatGPT, Claude, Llama...). It can write in many languages, more or less well.

Prompt

The text we send to it: the instruction. A good prompt = a role, constraints, examples, an answer format.

Few-shot

Putting a few examples in the prompt ("here are 4 sentences of the wanted style"). Zero-shot = no example.

Temperature

A creativity setting: 0 = stable answer, 1 = varied. High to generate, 0 to classify.

3 · The notebook, section by sectionAnatomy of the generation prompt

You are a native speaker of Algerian Arabic (Darija) and a linguist.      ← ROLE
Generate 10 short Algerian Darija sentences whose content expresses a POSITIVE feeling. ← TASK
Constraints: ~40% Arabic script, ~30% Arabizi, ~30% FR/AR code-switching.
Vary the topics. No pure MSA, no duplicates, 6 to 25 words.
No names of real people. Harmless content.                     ← CONSTRAINTS
Examples of the expected style: - gold sentence 1 ... - gold sentence 4      ← FEW-SHOT
Answer ONLY with a JSON array of 10 strings.                              ← FORMAT
  • Role: orients the style toward Darija.
  • Constraints: avoid standard Arabic and ensure variety.
  • Few-shot: gives the expected "taste".
  • JSON format: lets us read the answer automatically.

3 · The notebook, section by sectionSection 5: from sentence to numbers

A model does not read words: it only handles numbers. We must therefore turn each sentence into a list of numbers.

Simple idea: count the words

"bzaf mli7a bzaf" → bzaf:2, mli7a:1

Problem: "mli7a" ≠ "mliha" ≠ "مليحة". Every variant is an unknown word.

Our solution: count pieces of letters

"mli7a" → ml · li · i7 · 7a · mli · li7 · i7a · …

"mli7a" and "mliha" share "ml", "mli", "li": the model sees the resemblance.

TfidfVectorizer(analyzer="char_wb", ngram_range=(2, 5), sublinear_tf=True)
#   char_wb       = pieces of letters, inside each word
#   (2, 5)        = pieces of 2, 3, 4 and 5 letters
#   sublinear_tf  = if a piece appears 10 times, it does not count 10 times more

3 · The notebook, section by sectionTF-IDF: weighting the useful pieces

TF — frequency in the sentence

How many times the piece appears in this sentence. With sublinear_tf: 1 + ln(tf).

IDF — rarity in the corpus

A piece present everywhere ("a␣") says little; a rare piece ("zaf") says a lot.

idf = ln((1+N)/(1+df)) + 1

Example (N = 4 sentences)df (sentences containing it)idfInterpretation
"7a"3ln(5/4)+1 = 1.22frequent → low weight
"zaf"1ln(5/2)+1 = 1.92rare → high weight

Final weight = TF × IDF, then each sentence is normalised (length 1). Result: one sentence = one long vector of numbers.

3 · The notebook, section by sectionLogistic regression without difficult maths

1. One score per class

For each class, the model adds up the weights of the pieces of the sentence:

positive: 2.0 · neutral: 0.5 · negative: −1.0

2. Turn them into probabilities (softmax)

e^2.0 = 7.39 · e^0.5 = 1.65 · e^−1 = 0.37

total 9.41 → positive 79% · neutral 18% · negative 4%

3. Learning (.fit)

At the beginning the weights are arbitrary. The model looks at the training sentences with their labels, measures its mistakes, and adjusts the weights to make fewer. It repeats until stable (max_iter=2000 = maximum number of rounds). C=5: the larger C is, the more the model sticks to the training data (risk of "learning by heart").

4 · Evaluating correctlyTraining versus test

The exam analogy

If we give a student the exam answers while he revises, he gets 20/20 without understanding. The same for a model: we never test it on the sentences used to train it.

Classic error (data leakage): training and testing on the same sentences → misleading score (e.g. 100%).
Good practice: separate training (the model learns) from test (the model is examined on new material).
In our project: the corpus and the gold share no sentence (checked), and in A and D the gold is cut into folds (next slide).

4 · Evaluating correctlyThe metrics

Accuracy

Share of correct answers: correct / total. E.g. 41 out of 48 = 85%. At random: 33% (3 classes), 50% (2 classes).

Precision (of a class)

When the model says "positive", is it right? TP / (TP + FP)

Recall (of a class)

Among the truly positive sentences, how many does it find? TP / (TP + FN)

F1 and macro F1

F1 = harmonic mean of precision and recall = 2·P·R / (P+R). Macro F1 = average of the F1 of all classes.

TP = true positive (correctly predicted "positive"). FP = false positive (predicted "positive" wrongly). FN = false negative (a truly positive sentence missed).

Score on the generated corpus

Compared with the label requested in the prompt. If it is much higher than on the gold, the corpus is too easy or too stereotyped.

Score on the human gold

Compared with the reference labels. This is the most reliable measure (if the gold is reviewed by humans).

6 · ResultsSentiment: reading the numbers

A · gold only
B · templates → gold
C · LLM corpus → gold
D · gold + LLM corpus
  • A = 35% (chance: 33%): 36 training sentences are too few to learn 3 classes.
  • B = 69%: even a poor corpus helps, because it contains typical words ("top", "nul"...).
  • C = 85%: the more varied LLM corpus learns better.
  • D = 79%: < C, but the gap (≈ 3 sentences out of 48) is within the margin of error: we conclude nothing.

6 · ResultsDisinformation: the style trap

A · gold only
B · templates → gold
C · LLM corpus → gold
D · gold + LLM corpus
100% with templates: it is an alarm signal. Fake news items written (by a human or an LLM) contain obvious markers: "عاجل / URGENT", "شاركوا / partagez", "مصدر مجهول". Real news items contain "حسب بيان / selon l'APS". The model learns these style keywords.

Honest conclusion: our model detects the tone of a fake news, not the truth. A real fake news written soberly, or a real news with an alarmist tone, would fool it.

6 · ResultsStatistical uncertainty

Small sample = blurry numbers

With 48 test sentences: 1 sentence = 2.1 points. An 85% could in reality be 75% or 95%.

margin ≈ 1.96 × √(p(1−p)/n)

Scoren testmargin (95%)
Sentiment C: 85%48≈ ± 10 pts
Sentiment A: 35%48≈ ± 14 pts
Disinfo C: 95%20≈ ± 10 pts or more
Disinfo A: 55%20≈ ± 22 pts
Simple rule: if two scores differ by less than the margin, do not say one is better. The large gaps (35% → 85%) are reliable; the small ones (79% versus 85%) are not.

6 · ResultsThe limits, honestly

  • Sizes: gold of 48 / 20 sentences; corpora of 90 / 50 sentences.
  • Same "hand": gold and LLM corpus were drafted with the help of an LLM, in the same spirit → easier test than real TikTok or Facebook Darija.
  • Synthetic labels: it is the intention of the prompt. Not checked by several humans (no agreement measure such as kappa).
  • Disinformation: we detect the style, not the truth.
  • Simple classifier: does not understand negation, irony, context.
  • A single generation: little diversity of topics and formulations.
Easy improvement: review the gold, add real collected sentences (with permission), have 100 sentences annotated by 2 people, test XLM-R / DziriBERT.

8 · The juryLikely questions — 1/2

"Who wrote the corpus?"

An LLM (Claude), with the prompt of the function build_prompt. The gold was drafted with the help of an LLM and then reviewed by us.

"Why character n-grams?"

No standard spelling: "mli7a / mliha / مليحة" share pieces of letters.

"Are synthetic labels reliable?"

Not guaranteed: it is the intention of the prompt. Hence the reference gold, and the proposal to annotate a sample with several people.

"Why only 35% for A?"

36 training sentences for 3 classes: too few. This is exactly the problem the generated corpus solves (85%).

"Why is D worse than C?"

The gap (≈ 3 sentences out of 48) is within the margin of error (±10 points): we cannot conclude.

"What is cross-validation?"

We cut the gold in 4; each quarter serves once as the test, the other 3 as training. No sentence is tested by a model that has seen it.

8 · The juryLikely questions — 2/2

"Why 100% in disinformation?"

Fake news have a style (urgency, "share", vague source): the model learns the tone, not the truth. A high score proves nothing about the facts.

"Why not a stronger model?"

The baseline is deliberately simple to understand what happens; comparing with XLM-R or a few-shot LLM is the logical next step (not done here).

"Do LLMs know Darija?"

Partially: they often produce a standardised Darija; arabizi and code-switching are more fragile. This is why the prompt imposes a mix of scripts.

"Ethics?"

Harmless content, no real people; the fake news items are fictional and only serve for evaluation.

"Your contribution?"

A reproducible pipeline + a 4-case evaluation (A–D) + an honest error analysis on the limits.

"Link with a thesis?"

Synthetic data for low-resource languages; possible applications: moderation, psychological support in dialect.
